SHAP: SHapley Additive exPlanations

Explainable AI (XAI) bridges the gap between high-performance machine learning models and human-centric HR decision-making. Rooted in cooperative game theory (Lloyd Shapley, Nobel Laureate 2012), SHAP computes the marginal contribution of each employee feature to guarantee fair, locally accurate, and consistent explanations.

Global Interpretability
1. Global Interpretability

Global interpretability explains the overarching mechanics of the machine learning model across the entire workforce population:

  • Identifies macroscopic organizational turnover drivers (e.g., OverTime, MonthlyIncome, and JobRole).
  • Helps People Ops and HR executives formulate macro policies (e.g., overtime capping, compensation restructuring).
  • Detects model drift or structural biases before deployment.
2. Local (Instance-Level) Interpretability

Local interpretability explains why the model made a specific prediction for an individual employee:

  • Decomposes an individual's probability into specific positive risk drivers and negative protective buffers.
  • Powers interactive Waterfall Plots showing the journey from baseline expectation to final prediction.
  • Enables individualized, empathetic retention interventions rather than generic assumptions.

Mathematical Foundations of Shapley Attribution

In cooperative game theory, given a set of features \( F \) and a model prediction function \( f(S) \), the unique Shapley attribution \( \phi_i \) assigned to feature \( i \) is mathematically defined as:

$$\phi_i = \sum_{S \subseteq F \setminus \{i\}} \frac{|S|!\ (|F| - |S| - 1)!}{|F|!} \Big[ f(S \cup \{i\}) - f(S) \Big]$$

This mathematical formulation satisfies four fundamental axioms: Efficiency (sum of attributions equals model difference from expectation), Symmetry (equal contributors receive equal values), Dummy/Null Player (non-contributing features receive zero attribution), and Additivity.
