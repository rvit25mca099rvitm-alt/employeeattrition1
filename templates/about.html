Master of Computer Applications (MCA) Major Project

Employee Attrition Analysis and Prediction Using Explainable AI

An end-to-end Machine Learning and Explainable AI (XAI) decision-support system designed to forecast employee voluntary turnover risk, explain underlying organizational drivers via SHAP, and recommend evidence-based HR retention strategies.

Limitations of Existing Traditional Systems
  • Reactive Exit Interviews: Interventions happen after an employee has already resigned, making retention impossible.
  • Heuristic Gut Feeling: Managers rely on subjective opinions rather than empirical, multidimensional workforce patterns.
  • Black-Box Predictions: Legacy AI tools output binary scores without explaining why an employee is at risk.
  • Broad-Brush Policies: Organizations apply blanket salary or perk increases without diagnosing specific friction points.
Advantages of Proposed Explainable AI Solution
  • Proactive Forecasting: Flags elevated attrition probability before resignation occurs, allowing timely intervention.
  • True SHAP Explainability: Calculates exact mathematical Shapley contributions for both risk drivers and protective factors.
  • Responsible Decision Support: Adheres to ethical guidelines by providing guidance rather than automated employment actions.
  • Evidence-Based Retention: Automatically translates top SHAP risk drivers into tailored, actionable HR recommendations.

MCA Viva Voce Defense & Technical Q&A Guide
Key questions frequently asked by external examiners and academic evaluators

The benchmark IBM HR dataset contains 1,233 staying employees and only 237 leaving employees (~16.1% attrition rate). A trivial, naive model that predicts "Nobody ever leaves" would achieve an 83.9% accuracy while failing to catch a single exiting employee! In organizational retention, False Negatives (failing to identify an employee who resigns) are significantly costlier than False Positives. Therefore, models must be evaluated on Recall, Precision, F1-score, and ROC-AUC rather than raw accuracy.

Traditional Gini importance or feature weights only provide a global aggregate score, suffer from bias toward continuous/high-cardinality features, and cannot explain individual predictions. In contrast, SHAP (rooted in cooperative game theory) computes local, instance-specific Shapley values that add up exactly to the difference between the base model output and the individual prediction (\(\sum \phi_i = f(x) - E[f(x)]\)). Furthermore, SHAP reveals both the magnitude and the direction (whether a feature increases or decreases attrition risk) for each individual employee.

Data leakage was prevented by performing a stratified 80/20 train-test split before fitting any transformers. The ColumnTransformer (incorporating StandardScaler and OneHotEncoder) was fitted exclusively on the training partition (X_train). The test partition and new user inputs are transformed using the previously fitted parameters without refitting. Constant columns (EmployeeCount, Over18, StandardHours) and identifier columns (EmployeeNumber) were removed to avoid artificial shortcuts.

Under Responsible AI principles:
  1. Predictions are explicitly presented as statistical estimates, not deterministic facts.
  2. The system explicitly warns that SHAP establishes correlation, not causation.
  3. The system must never be used to automate employment termination, denial of promotion, or punitive actions.
  4. All outputs emphasize human oversight, manager empathy, and constructive retention support.
